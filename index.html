Learn the Ship Playbook with Phoebe

A gym chain came out of a planning round with twelve candidate use cases, every one of them wanted by somebody. Half of them could not be built at all, because the data they needed did not exist, and the estimate charged for them anyway: sixty-four weeks of plan, of which thirty-seven bought nothing. The single most-requested item on the list needed no data, cleared every check in four weeks, and moved the money the business makes by exactly nothing. And the thing that unlocked the most value on the whole board was a free-text box the front desk already fills in about a third of the time. Six sessions on the four moves a roadmap needs, in order, so that writing one for a particular industry means filling in a frame rather than re-deriving it.

The six sessions 🎯 · the spine a vertical roadmap imports

The running situation is Ridgeline Fitness, an invented 22-site gym chain that makes its money on recurring memberships. Twelve candidates go through two gates: whether the business holds the data, and whether the case moves the money that particular business makes. Six clear the first gate. One of the six is worth nothing.

🪜 Session 1 · advanced

The five categories

See it, explain it, predict it, decide it, do it. A ladder rather than a menu, because each rung eats the one below it. Skip a rung and nothing errors: it ships and becomes confidently wrong.

▶ Start here ⚖️ Session 2 · advanced

The four-way verdict

Automate it, augment the person, build something new, keep it human. A separate axis from the category, passed on a task and never on a job, and there is a published argument that the whole field leans toward one of the four for reasons unrelated to which is right.

task, never the job 🎛️ Session 3 · bench night

The verdict bench

Twelve candidates, five ways to pick, every number computed in front of you. Dropping what cannot be built takes 64 weeks to 27 with the value unchanged, and the most-requested item on the list scores zero.

★ the bench 🚪 Session 4 · advanced

The data gate

Held in full, held partly, not held at all, and the middle one is what wastes projects because the query runs and the answer looks like an answer. Plus how to tell a form change from a standing job before agreeing to either.

the unlock table 💰 Session 5 · advanced

The revenue model decides

The same use case is worth different amounts at different businesses. Also: the three failure-rate statistics everybody quotes to justify a roadmap, and why none of them survives being looked up.

What is honest here 🧾

Two of the four moves rest on published work that was read at source. One rests on nothing published at all, and session 5 says so in plain words rather than borrowing a citation that does not fit.
